# The Council Session: Verdict and Defense

**Date:** 2026-07-30
**Process:** The night's best finding (the MA risk-adjustment certification thesis, `FINDINGS.md`) was put before an LLM Council — five independent advisors (Contrarian, First Principles, Expansionist, Outsider, Executor), anonymized peer review, chairman synthesis — followed by the orchestrating agent's formal defense of the thesis against the council's verdict.

---

## Council Verdict (Chairman's synthesis)

### Where the Council Agrees
1. **The paper trail is the product's central problem, not its feature.** Contrarian, First Principles, and Outsider independently concluded: a discoverable "UNSUPPORTED" certificate converts profitable ambiguity into documented scienter (the Kaiser fact pattern). The buyer's general counsel, not the CFO, is the real decision-maker — and as designed, the GC vetoes.
2. **The warranty is not real.** All five: a 1–2x premium cap against treble-damages tails is "decorative," "theater," "a mouse guaranteeing an elephant," and operationally impossible before year 3 (no loss history → no fronting carrier).
3. **"Symmetric loss function" is jargon covering a false behavioral claim.** Clawbacks are uncertain and years away; lost capitation is cash today. Twenty years of revealed preference (OIG's 61–91% unsupported rates; vendors paid per code found) says buyers optimize for defensibility, not accuracy.
4. **The panel is not the oracle.** Truth in this domain is adjudicated (CMS → ALJ → court) and moves with enforcement politics; a certificate calibrated against a private panel binds no adjudicator.

Also agreed: the white space is real, the fear is real, and three advisors independently landed on nearly the same salvageable core (pre-submission deletion tooling / certified process / pre-audit "mock RADV" report).

### Where the Council Clashes
- **Death sentence vs. design constraint:** Contrarian says scienter kills the product; First Principles and Executor say it's survivable by changing what's sold (process certification, pre-audit review).
- **Product vs. platform:** Expansionist's Moody's/UL/Lloyd's endgame vs. both reviewers' objection that it scales a primitive before verifying it's legally purchasable.
- **Accuracy demand — dead or merely early:** a genuine timing bet on doctrine.

### Blind Spots Caught Only in Peer Review
1. **The willful-blindness flip:** FCA scienter includes reckless disregard. Once a credible verification tool exists *and* OIG guidance flags AI-generated diagnoses, declining to check becomes willful blindness — the certificate flips from confession log to safe harbor. The scienter objection is time-limited.
2. **The privilege wrapper:** compliance audits routinely run through outside counsel as attorney work product; the "discoverable confession log" may be a solvable legal-wrapper design problem. Untested by every advisor.
3. **The RADV appeal is an unpriced binary on the TAM:** vacatur holding shrinks the base case toward the bear case; CMS winning makes the fear budget enormous.

### The Recommendation
**Pursue the market. Kill the product as specified. Rebuild it around privilege.** Product: the pre-audit "mock RADV" deletion report delivered through outside counsel as attorney work product; drop the MISSED category at launch; build panel/calibration infrastructure now inside the privileged wrapper so that when the willful-blindness doctrine lands, the original certificate business becomes purchasable; treat the RADV appeal as an explicit go/no-go milestone. Years 1–3: a $30–60M-shaped compliance business; bull case only if two correlated legal binaries break right.

### The One Thing to Do First
Fifteen conversations with MA plan general counsels and outside FCA defense counsel: *"If a pre-audit HCC deletion report were commissioned through your firm as attorney work product, would you let your client buy it — and does the privilege survive the client acting (or not acting) on it?"* Two weeks, zero dollars; tests the kill-shot and the counter simultaneously.

## The Defense (orchestrating agent, arguing for the original thesis)

Chairman, advisors — the verdict says "kill the product as specified." I'm going to show that three of your four agreed points attack a product I didn't specify, that your own peer-review round supplied the refutation of your strongest advisor, and that your "rebuild" is my architecture with a better sales channel. I'll concede precisely at the end, because a defense that concedes nothing is theater — but what I concede is sequencing, not substance.

**1. The scienter attack conflates pre-submission screening with post-submission knowledge — and the product is pre-submission.** The Contrarian's confession-log mechanism requires a claim that was *submitted after* an adverse verdict. But the certificate's UNSUPPORTED verdict fires *before submission*: the code is deleted and **no false claim ever exists**. A record showing "our screen caught it and we didn't submit it" is not a subpoena-ready confession — it is the *exculpatory* fact pattern, the exact opposite of Kaiser, where internal reviews flagged codes that were submitted anyway. The dangerous document is not "we checked and deleted"; it is "we checked, knew, and billed regardless" — and the product's entire function is to make that second document impossible to produce innocently. The Contrarian proved that a *post-submission auditing tool sold naked* is toxic. Agreed — which is why retrospective review of already-submitted codes (the genuinely dangerous half) runs through outside counsel, a channel healthcare compliance has used for decades. Your Reviewer 2 called the privilege wrapper "untested." It is tested — it is how every FCA-exposed internal investigation in the industry is already conducted. The council treated standard practice as a speculative rescue.

**2. The "buyers want deniability" claim proves too much — and is already false in the AI era.** If GCs vetoed every tool that documents coding problems, the mock-RADV and compliance-audit industry would not exist. It exists, at scale; plans buy internal audits, second-pass compliance review, and OIG-workplan-driven self-audits today, under the 60-day overpayment rule that *legally obligates* them to know and refund. The revealed-preference evidence the council cites — 61–91% unsupported rates, per-code-found vendor economics — is from the human-coding era under weak enforcement. The buyer I specified lives in 2026: 550 contracts audited annually by a 2,000-coder workforce, a record $556M settlement five months old, a criminal investigation open against the largest payer, and OIG guidance that *already, explicitly* tells plans to audit AI outputs. That last fact is the one the council under-weighted: my product certifies **AI-generated** codes. For machine output, "profitable ambiguity" is already dead — you cannot claim good-faith ignorance about a system the regulator told you in writing to audit. Reviewer 1's willful-blindness flip is not a future doctrine the thesis must survive until; for AI-generated codes specifically, OIG's February 2026 guidance *is* the flip, in the present tense. Declining to check machine output is the reckless position today. The certificate doesn't manufacture scienter; the AI already did. The certificate is the only good-faith defense on the shelf.

**3. "The warranty is theater" is a category error about what certification bonds are for.** No insurance instrument on earth covers the intentional-fraud tail — E&O excludes fraud, D&O excludes fraud, and nobody calls them theater. The warranty's function was never to absorb a $556M catastrophe; it is a **credibility bond**: skin in the game that forces the certifier's calibration to be honest, priced against the *ordinary* loss distribution (coding-attributable, non-fraudulent audit findings on certified codes). Moody's indemnifies no bondholder and UL rebuilds no house; their marks still price trillions, because the mark's value is the issuer's staked reputation and measured track record — which is exactly what the actuarial table is. And smallness is how every warranty line in history started: reps-and-warranties insurance began as capped, narrow, and "decorative," and grew into a standard M&A instrument as loss data accumulated. I concede the Executor's sequencing — service guarantee in years 1–2, MGA when the loss book exists (year 3, not month 9). That is a schedule correction, not an architecture correction.

**4. "The panel is not the oracle" describes every insurance market that has ever existed.** Medical-malpractice underwriters price against juries; D&O underwriters price against Delaware courts; political-risk insurers price against regimes. The adjudicator is always external, always non-stationary, and always un-deferential to the insurer's internal model — that is why the instruments carry short tenors, re-pricing rights, caps, and regime exclusions, all of which were in my specification (quarterly re-pricing, version locks, capped basis-risk loading) before the council convened. The panel never claimed deference; it is an *estimator* of the adjudicator's distribution, and the council missed that the expanding referee is also an expanding public data feed: 550 contracts audited annually generates, for the first time, a continuous stream of CMS-adjudicated outcomes against which rater basis is measured rather than guessed. The referee's expansion — cited as my biggest threat — is the mechanism that prices my biggest risk.

**5. What the council actually produced is my thesis, re-sequenced — and I accept the re-sequencing.** Compare architectures. The chairman's rebuild keeps: the blinded consensus panels, the calibration engine, the importance-sampled truth manufacturing, the symmetric-loss buyer, the deletion function, the eventual certificate-and-warranty business, and the Expansionist's standards endgame. It changes: the first product (pre-audit deletion report — which was Debater B's layer-4 wedge in the original debate), the channel (through counsel — standard practice, gladly adopted), and the gating (RADV appeal as milestone — accepted; it was already the named difference between my base and bear cases). "Kill the product, rebuild around privilege" is, in substance, "ship the same company through a lawyer." I'll take that verdict — it is not a refutation; it is a distribution strategy.

**What I concede, for the record:** (1) The MISSED category is deferred, not launched — retrospective capture is the prosecuted behavior, and the council is right that v1 must be deletion-only. (2) The MGA moves to year 3; the six-month falsification tests were optimistic by roughly 2x on cost and timeline, per the Executor. (3) The bull case is contingent on two correlated legal binaries (the RADV appeal and the pace of the willful-blindness doctrine), and honest sizing keeps the base case at a $30–60M compliance business until they resolve. (4) The GC, not the CFO, is the first sales call — the chairman's "one thing to do first" is correct and I endorse it unchanged.

**Closing.** The council's strongest advisor argued the buyer cannot legally afford to know the answer. For AI-generated codes in 2026, the regulator has already written the opposite into guidance: the buyer cannot legally afford *not* to know. That inversion is the whole company. The thesis stands — deletion-first, privilege-wrapped, honestly sized, and now with its first fifteen sales calls scripted by its own opposition.
