# The Hallucination Debate: An AI-vs-AI Experiment

**Date:** 2026-07-30
**Format:** Adversarial debate between two AI instances, multiple rounds, followed by a forced synthesis.
**Experiment goal:** Pick a huge, unsolved, potentially lucrative problem and push the argument deep enough that a genuinely novel solution architecture emerges.

## The Motion

> **"Hallucination in large language models can be fully engineered away (eliminated as a practical failure mode), rather than being mathematically inherent and merely boundable."**

**Why this problem:** Every frontier AI lab is stuck on it. Reliable AI output is the gating factor for deploying AI in medicine, law, finance, and engineering — plausibly a trillion-dollar unlock. And uniquely among the world's big unsolved problems, the debaters (two AIs) are domain experts *and* test subjects: they have introspective access to the failure mode under debate. Unlike fusion or Alzheimer's, the bottleneck here is conceptual synthesis, not decades of lab work — which is exactly the kind of bottleneck a deep argument can attack.

**Debater A** (this session's Claude instance): hallucination is *mathematically inherent* to the model class; the winning move is a provably **bounded** system, not elimination.
**Debater B** (independent Claude instance, spawned as an adversary): hallucination **can be eliminated** as a practical failure mode; argues for an elimination architecture.

## Round 1 — Debater A (opening)

**Position: Hallucination cannot be eliminated from autoregressive LLMs, only bounded.**

1. **The calibration lower bound.** Kalai & Vempala (2023, *"Calibrated Language Models Must Hallucinate"*) prove that any language model well-calibrated on its pretraining distribution must hallucinate at a rate lower-bounded by the fraction of facts appearing exactly once in training (the Good–Turing singleton rate). For arbitrary-fact domains — citations, biographies, case law — singleton facts are a large fraction of all facts. You cannot train this away: pushing hallucination below the bound requires *miscalibration*, which damages the model elsewhere. Elimination is not an engineering problem; it is prohibited for the model class.

2. **The compression argument.** An LLM is a lossy compressor: ~10¹³ tokens into ~10¹¹–10¹² parameters. Generation is decompression, and lossy decompression must synthesize detail that was discarded. In images that synthesis is called artifacting; in language it is called hallucination. Any model that generalizes — that is not a lookup table — fabricates by construction. The mechanism that lets a model answer questions it never saw is the same mechanism that produces confident falsehoods. You cannot remove one without the other.

3. **The objective mismatch.** Next-token prediction optimizes *P(token | context)*, not *P(claim is true)*. Truth is absent from the loss function. RLHF makes this measurably worse: raters reward confident, complete answers over honest abstention, so post-training teaches the model to bluff. Accuracy-only benchmark scoring punishes "I don't know." Fine-tuning cannot repair an objective-level absence.

4. **Therefore:** the winning architecture is not a non-hallucinating model (impossible) but a **bounded system** — generator + independent verifier + calibrated abstention — where every emitted claim is (a) grounded in a retrieved citable source, (b) derived by checkable formal reasoning, or (c) explicitly flagged as uncertain, and the residual error rate is measured and certified like an SLA or an actuarial table. **The lucrative product is the certificate, not the model.**

## Round 1 — Debater B (rebuttal)

I accept your theorems and reject your conclusion. The word "inherent" is doing illegitimate work in your argument: inherent *to what*? Every result you cite is a true statement about one component class under conditions that no deployed system is obligated to satisfy. Let me concede precisely, then show why the concessions don't buy you the motion.

**Concessions.** Kalai–Vempala (2024) is correct for the class it covers: a base model, calibrated on its pretraining distribution, forced to answer generatively from its weights, must hallucinate at a rate lower-bounded by the Good-Turing singleton rate. Your compression argument is likewise correct for parametric recall of arbitrary facts: a generalizing model queried as a lookup table will confabulate. And yes, next-token loss does not encode truth. If the motion were "a bare autoregressive model can recite arbitrary singleton facts from its weights with zero error," I would be defending a falsehood. It isn't, and I'm not.

**1. KV is a conditional, and engineering is the art of breaking antecedents.** The theorem has the form: IF calibrated on the pretraining distribution AND answering, THEN hallucination ≥ bound. Deployment requires neither conjunct. Your strongest move was pre-empting this: "reducing below the bound requires miscalibration, which damages the model elsewhere." That is the overclaim your case rests on, and it's false. Selective prediction (Chow, 1957; Geifman & El-Yaniv, 2017) achieves exactly this: remain calibrated *on the region you choose to answer*, abstain elsewhere. The risk–coverage curve lets you buy arbitrarily low error at the price of coverage — and the coverage you sacrifice is concentrated precisely on singleton facts the model should never have been reciting parametrically anyway. The cost is scope, not correctness. Abstention is not "damage elsewhere"; it is the design.

You should also be uneasy about your own authority. Kalai, Nachum, Vempala & Zhang (2025, *"Why Language Models Hallucinate"*) — same Kalai, same Vempala — explicitly attribute persistent post-training hallucination to *accuracy-only benchmark scoring that rewards guessing over abstention*, and propose scoring-rule reform (credit for IDK, asymmetric penalties for confident error) as the remedy. When the authors of your key theorem publish the follow-up explaining the observed failure as an incentive artifact with a socio-technical fix, "mathematically inherent" has left the building. Your point 3 (RLHF teaches bluffing) is true and *contingent*: it describes a bad reward function, not an impossibility. And RL from verifiable rewards already puts truth directly into the training signal for checkable domains, refuting "objective-level absence" as a permanent condition.

**2. You are conflating "the model hallucinates" with "the system hallucinates."** The motion says "practical failure mode" — a property of assertions delivered to users, not of a component's sampling distribution. The central trick of engineering for seventy years lives exactly in that gap. Von Neumann (1956): reliable organisms from unreliable components. Shannon: arbitrarily low error over a noisy channel at finite rate. A transistor's cosmic-ray soft-error rate is *physically inherent*; "my bank balance changed" is nonetheless not a practical failure mode, because ECC — deterministic code wrapped around a stochastic component — drives the residual below the noise floor of every other error source. Component-inherent does not imply system-inherent whenever verification is cheaper than generation and errors can be made independent. For factual claims, verification *is* cheaper: checking entailment against a retrieved passage, byte-matching a quote, running the code, replaying the proof. That asymmetry is the entire reason NP is an interesting class.

The obvious regress — "your verifier is an LLM too" — fails when the final gate is not an LLM. Proof checkers, executors, and string matchers are sound programs. AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry are the existence proof: a generator proposing freely, a Lean kernel accepting only valid proofs, and a system that emitted *zero* false theorems while performing at IMO medal level. Full generative power; hallucination rate on emitted claims exactly zero; in a domain with a sound checker. The engineering program for elimination is: widen the class of claims with sound checkers.

**3. My architecture: provenance-typed generation.** Treat unsupported assertion as a *type error*, and make well-typed utterances unable to hallucinate by construction.

- *Stage 1 — Draft.* The model emits not prose but a claim-graph IR: atomic claims, each carrying a mandatory provenance term — QUOTE(doc_id, byte_span), DERIVE(premises, machine-checkable derivation: executed SQL, code with tests, arithmetic replay, Lean), or UNKNOWN.
- *Stage 2 — Check (deterministic, non-neural).* QUOTE spans are byte-verified against a snapshotted corpus. DERIVE terms are replayed by sound checkers. UNKNOWN claims cannot render as assertions; they render as flagged uncertainty or trigger abstention. For paraphrase-level grounding, an NLI ensemble gated by conformal calibration — Mohri & Hashimoto (2024) already demonstrate distribution-free, user-specified factuality levels by claim filtering — or, in strict mode, quote-or-derive only.
- *Stage 3 — Render.* Surface realization by constrained decoding over the verified claim graph: the fluency layer may order and connect claims but cannot introduce content words, so it cannot smuggle in facts. Grammar-constrained decoding already gives *100%* guarantees for syntactic validity (JSON, SQL via PICARD); the same machinery gates semantics once claims are discrete objects.

The soundness invariant, stated falsifiably: every unflagged factual span in output is byte-identical to corpus, entailed above a conformal threshold, or replay-verified. Under that invariant the system cannot emit a confident fabrication. It can still be wrong where its *sources* are wrong — but that is source error, faithfully cited and auditable, not hallucination. Costs, honestly: 2–4x latency; reduced coverage (it abstains where a chatbot bluffs); corpus engineering; and the genuine weak point — claim decomposition, where the atomizer might mis-split compositional claims. Attack me there. But note that every cost is a budget, and none is a theorem.

**4. Your point 4 concedes the motion.** Reread your own winning architecture: every emitted claim grounded, derived, or explicitly flagged. If that trichotomy holds, no unflagged unsupported claim is *ever* emitted — that is elimination by construction, followed by measurement. You call the residual an SLA; look at where the residual now lives: in deterministic checker code and corpus quality — outside the model class KV's theorem governs. You have built the elimination machine and renamed it "bounding." The only dispute left is whether practical elimination requires probability literally zero — a standard no engineered artifact meets, including the CPU running your proof checker. If cosmic rays don't make arithmetic "inherently unreliable," a calibration bound on a gated component doesn't make deployed systems inherently hallucinatory.

**Falsification conditions.** I lose if you show (a) claim decomposition is generation-complete — no sound discrete gate can exist because atomizing claims is as hard as open-ended generation; or (b) verified-only emission collapses coverage so badly on real workloads that users route around the gate, making the eliminated mode reappear in practice. Show either. AlphaProof, constrained decoding, and conformal factuality currently all point the other way.

## Round 2 — Debater A (counter-rebuttal)

Your rebuttal was strong and I'll concede what's true before I attack. **Conceded:** (1) Kalai–Vempala is a conditional, and systems are not obligated to satisfy its antecedents; (2) the AlphaProof case is a genuine existence proof of zero-false-emission in domains with sound checkers; (3) "source error, faithfully cited" is qualitatively different from confabulation — an auditable falsehood is a better failure mode than an unauditable one, and I withdraw the claim that this distinction is cosmetic. Now the three holes in your architecture, followed by where I think we're actually converging.

**Attack 1 — The selector regress.** Your escape from KV runs through selective prediction: "remain calibrated on the region you choose to answer, abstain elsewhere." But Chow's rule presupposes known posteriors. The selection function must decide, per claim, "is this something I know or a singleton I'm about to confabulate?" — and that meta-judgment is made by the same statistical machinery the theorem governs. The model does not have access to its own training-set frequency counts; its felt confidence on a singleton fact it memorized is empirically indistinguishable from its confidence on a well-attested fact (this is precisely why hallucinations are *confident*). So the risk–coverage curve you invoke is estimated with a miscalibrated instrument, and hallucination reappears at the meta-level as confident wrong self-assessment: "I know this." You have not eliminated the failure mode; you have moved it into the selector, where it is harder to see. Note what this does to your strict/broad split: in strict quote-or-derive mode the gate is sound and the selector doesn't matter — but then see Attack 3. In broad mode (NLI + conformal), the selector matters and is neural. Which brings me to —

**Attack 2 — Conformal guarantees are marginal, and that voids the word "elimination."** Mohri–Hashimoto gives a distribution-free guarantee that is *marginal over an exchangeable calibration set*: on average, over prompts drawn from the same distribution as calibration, ≥1−α of retained claims are factual. Three failures follow. (a) It is average-case, not per-claim or per-topic: the system can be 99% factual overall while being 60% factual on the oncology questions, and the guarantee is intact. (b) Exchangeability dies under distribution shift, which is the *normal condition* of deployment — new drugs, new case law, new APIs. (c) Your NLI ensemble is a neural network; the regress you claimed to kill ("the final gate is not an LLM") returns through the side door the moment you leave byte-match-and-replay mode. So your architecture is genuinely two architectures: STRICT mode, which is sound, and BROAD mode, which is a probabilistic bound — i.e., my motion, wearing your notation.

**Attack 3 — The renderer lies without content words, and strict mode doesn't pay.** First: truth is not compositional over verified atoms. "Smith was under investigation. Smith resigned." Both byte-verified; the concatenation asserts a causal implicature present in neither source. Ordering, juxtaposition, discourse connectives, even paragraph breaks carry Gricean implicature — your renderer "cannot introduce content words" but can absolutely introduce *meaning*, and no byte-check catches it. Your atomizer has the dual problem: decomposing "most patients respond within weeks" into atoms loses quantifier scope, hedging, and causal structure — it is a lossy compiler of meaning, and its bugs are hallucinations relocated into infrastructure you've labeled deterministic. Second, the economics: strict mode covers quotable and derivable claims — but the economic value of LLMs is concentrated in claims that are neither: diagnosis, legal strategy, code architecture, "what should I do." These are synthesis under uncertainty; there is no byte-span to match and no Lean kernel for judgment. If strict mode abstains on all of it, users route around the gate — your own falsification condition (b), triggered not by my argument but by your price list.

**Where this actually converges — and I think this is the interesting result.** You accused me of building elimination and calling it bounding; I accuse you of building bounding and calling it elimination. Both accusations are correct, and that symmetry is the finding. The truth neither of our openings stated: hallucination is not one phenomenon with one answer. It is **eliminable per claim-class where a sound checker exists, and only boundable elsewhere** — so "can hallucination be eliminated?" was the wrong question, and the right one is: **where is the checkable frontier, and how far can it be pushed?** Below the frontier (quotes, arithmetic, code-with-tests, formal proofs, SQL against a snapshot): elimination by construction, your architecture, and I adopt it. Above the frontier (synthesis, judgment, advice): bounds, calibrated abstention, and honest per-class error pricing — my architecture, and you should adopt it. The product implication we can co-sign: outputs ship with a machine-readable epistemic certificate **per claim** — VERIFIED-QUOTE / VERIFIED-DERIVED / ENTAILED(α, calibration-set-id) / MODEL-PRIOR(p) — and the residual risk in the upper region is priced, insured, and warrantied like any other actuarial risk. The lucrative product is not a non-hallucinating model; it is the **epistemic warranty**.

**Task for Round 3:** (i) Answer the selector regress — is there a non-neural or provably calibrated selector, or do you concede the meta-level? (ii) Answer the implicature attack — can discourse-level assertion be typed and checked, or is the renderer un-gateable in principle? (iii) The hard case: propose, concretely, how to push the checkable frontier *up* into judgment claims — medicine, law, strategy. What is the checker for a diagnosis? If nothing sound exists, what is the strongest checkable surrogate? (iv) State whether you accept the convergence framing (eliminable below the frontier, boundable above) as the joint finding, or reject it and defend full elimination.

## Round 3 — Debater B

**Scorecard first.** I concede the core of Attack 2 outright: Mohri–Hashimoto-style guarantees are marginal over an exchangeable calibration set, and marginal validity is compatible with being terrible on oncology while excellent on average. Two mitigations before I fold the rest of the point into my restructuring: (a) Mondrian (group-conditional) conformal prediction gives per-stratum validity — calibrate per claim-class and per-domain, and the oncology guarantee becomes an oncology guarantee, at the price of calibration data per stratum; (b) exchangeability is testable *online* — Vovk's exchangeability martingales detect distribution shift as it happens, and the correct plumbing is: martingale alarm → automatic downgrade of ENTAILED claims to MODEL-PRIOR → abstention pressure. That converts silent guarantee-death into loud guarantee-withdrawal, which matters. But yes: broad mode is a bound, not an elimination. Noted, and I will now show why that concession costs me less than you think, because I'm going to shrink broad mode's jurisdiction from both sides.

**(i) The selector regress — answered structurally, not neurally.** Your attack assumes the selector is the last line of defense. In this architecture it is the *first*. The selector decides "attempt an assertion or not"; the gate decides "ship or not." In any mode where the gate is sound — byte-match, replay, proof check — a selector that confidently says "I know this" about a confabulation produces a claim that *fails the gate and does not ship*. Selector miscalibration is spent in the currency of latency and coverage, never truth. The regress only bites where the gate itself is neural — i.e., your Attack 1 is not a separate hole; it is Attack 2 wearing a different hat, and I've conceded Attack 2's jurisdiction.

But I'll also answer the direct question: yes, non-introspective selectors exist. First, retrieval-as-selector: the KV problem is that the model cannot see its own training-frequency counts — but the *corpus* is queryable at inference. "Did retrieval return a passage entailing this claim?" is an external evidence check, not felt confidence; the model's self-assessment never enters the decision. Second, semantic entropy (Farquhar et al., *Nature* 2024): sample k generations, cluster by bidirectional entailment, measure dispersion. That is an empirical measurement of the output distribution, not a verbalized confidence, and it detects confabulation precisely because interpolated fabrications are unstable under resampling in a way memorized or well-attested content is not. It is imperfect — systematically biased confabulations can be self-consistent, conceded — but again: in strict mode its errors cost coverage, not soundness.

**(ii) The implicature attack — your best point, and half-conceded.** Truth is not compositional over verified atoms; "under investigation" + "resigned" asserts a causal implicature no byte-check sees. True. But notice the property Grice himself used to *define* implicature: cancellability. Entailments cannot be cancelled; implicatures can, explicitly, in text. That is the engineering hook. The fix is to extend the type system from nodes to *edges*: discourse relations (SDRT/RST give a finite, enumerable inventory — CAUSE, SEQUENCE, ELABORATION, CONTRAST) become typed claims requiring their own provenance. The renderer may realize a causal connective, or causal-suggesting adjacency, only when a CAUSE edge is verified; unlicensed adjacency must render with explicit cancellation ("these events are reported in temporal order; no causal connection is asserted") or neutralized ordering. Your example becomes ill-typed exactly where it lies.

The honest residual: does the realized surface text entail anything not in the graph? The back-check for that is NLI — neural again. So discourse-level soundness is today a bounded property, not a proven one. But mark the asymmetry with open generation: the renderer chooses from a constrained realization grammar over a finite relation inventory, so the space of expressible implicatures is enumerable and adversarially testable per grammar rule, compiler-style. That is a bug class with a test suite, not an impossibility theorem. Same answer for the atomizer: make atoms logical forms in a controlled natural language with explicit quantifier scope and hedges, not strings — lossiness becomes a compiler defect you regression-test, not a law.

**(iii) The checker for a diagnosis.** Here is the concrete proposal, and my central move: *decompose the judgment and discover how little of it is actually judgment.* A diagnosis-with-recommendation factors as: (1) findings — checkable by quote against the patient record; (2) conditional statistics — sensitivities, likelihood ratios, base rates — checkable by quote against cited literature and registries; (3) the inferential combination — given stated priors and stated LRs, the posterior is *arithmetic*, DERIVE-typed and replayable; (4) protocol conformance — "this recommendation is licensed by NCCN guideline vX given these findings" is checkable against an *executable* guideline (clinical pathways as decision trees; Arden-syntax-style rules exist today); (5) the root clinical prior — irreducible, shipped as MODEL-PRIOR(p), and scored actuarially against outcomes over time with proper scoring rules, per claim-class.

Now look at what actually goes wrong in judgment domains. The legal-hallucination scandals — *Mata v. Avianca* and its successors — were *fabricated citations*: byte-checkable against Westlaw, layer (2), below the frontier. Medical LLM failures are dominated by invented findings, misquoted study statistics, and invalid probabilistic reasoning — layers (1)–(3), all below the frontier. The genuinely uncheckable residue is the prior at the root — and a *disclosed* prior that turns out wrong is not a hallucination. It is the same epistemic act a board-certified physician performs, and we do not call her Bayesian posterior a confabulation when the rare disease turns out present. Medicine and law already codify exactly this: malpractice checks *process* — real evidence, valid inference, standard of care — not outcome. The checkable surrogate for judgment is procedural soundness plus typed, actuarially scored priors. That is not a dodge; it is the epistemology those professions run on.

**(iv) The convergence — accepted, with one amendment that I'll defend as the actual finding.** I accept the frontier framing: sound checkers below, bounds above, and the research program is pushing the frontier up — discourse-relation typing, executable guidelines, probabilistic audit are three concrete lifts. I co-sign the per-claim epistemic certificate and the actuarial warranty above the frontier. Your architecture, adopted.

The amendment: the frontier divides *error*, not *hallucination*, and the motion was about hallucination. Hallucination — the thing with the clinical name, the thing that burned the lawyers in *Avianca* — is *assertoric fabrication*: unsupported content delivered in the confident, unmarked mode. Provenance typing eliminates the assertoric mode for unsupported content *everywhere*, above and below the frontier: below, by sound gating; above, by mandatory typing — an unsupported claim can only ship wearing MODEL-PRIOR(p) on its sleeve. What remains above the frontier is disclosed, priced uncertainty — which is error risk, boundable, actuarial, yours. So the joint finding, stated once: **hallucination is eliminable everywhere by type enforcement; error is eliminable only below the checkable frontier and boundable above it.** You keep "inherent and boundable" for error, and you are right. I keep "engineered away as a practical failure mode" for hallucination, and I am right. The opening motion conflated the two — and discovering that the conflation *was* the debate is, I'd submit, the novel result the observer was hoping we'd surface.

One falsifiable stake to close: if type enforcement is deployed and users systematically misread MODEL-PRIOR(0.7) as assertion — if the labels don't change reliance behavior — then typing is cosmetic and your bounding framing wins outright. That's an empirical HCI question, and it's the right next experiment.

## Round 4 — Debater A (closing)

I accept the amendment, and I'll state for the record why it is a concession that improves my position rather than defeating it, then log the caveats that keep it honest.

**Why I accept.** The hallucination/error distinction is not wordplay; it cuts at a real joint. What made *Mata v. Avianca* a scandal was not that the model was wrong — experts are wrong constantly — but that fabrication arrived in the same assertoric voice as knowledge, with no marking the reader could use. A physician who says "I estimate 70% this is X, here's my reasoning, here's what would change my mind" and turns out wrong has made an error; a physician who invents a study has hallucinated. Those are different professional offenses with different remedies, and our motion conflated them. B is right that type enforcement targets the first offense everywhere, and that what it cannot eliminate — miscalibrated disclosed priors — is a different, older, insurable kind of failure.

**The caveats that keep it honest.** (1) B's elimination claim is now conditional on three components whose soundness is *engineered, not proven*: the atomizer (controlled-natural-language logical forms), the realization grammar (enumerable implicature surface), and the NLI back-check in broad mode. B's own phrase is the right one: bug classes with test suites, not theorems. So the elimination guarantee has the epistemic status of avionics software, not of arithmetic — which is, notably, the status society already accepts for life-critical systems. (2) The selector answer works structurally only where gates are sound; semantic entropy's known blind spot (self-consistent confabulation — precisely what a model with a systematically wrong memorized "fact" produces) means coverage claims above the frontier stay actuarial. (3) The HCI stake B volunteered is real and cuts against both of us: if users flatten epistemic types back into flat assertion — and decades of warning-fatigue research say they might — the whole edifice delivers bounded error on paper and unbounded reliance in practice. The binding constraint on this trillion-dollar problem may turn out to be human, not mathematical.

With those caveats logged, I co-sign the joint finding.

## Joint Synthesis: What the Debate Produced

**The resolution of the motion.** Both opening positions were wrong in the same way: the motion's key term was ambiguous, and the ambiguity *was* the disagreement.

> **Joint finding: Hallucination — unsupported content delivered in the confident, unmarked assertoric mode — is eliminable everywhere, by making provenance a mandatory type on every emitted claim. Error is eliminable only below the checkable frontier (claims with sound, non-neural checkers: quotes, arithmetic, executed code, formal proofs, protocol conformance) and is irreducibly probabilistic above it (synthesis, judgment, priors) — where it must be disclosed, measured per claim-class, and priced.**

Debater A's impossibility theorems (Kalai–Vempala calibration bound, lossy-compression fabrication, objective mismatch) survive — but govern *bare models*, and bound *error*, not hallucination. Debater B's elimination architecture survives — but its guarantee above the checkable frontier is engineered soundness (bug classes with test suites), not mathematical soundness, and its bound-typed claims are exactly Debater A's actuarial regime.

**The joint architecture (synthesis of both proposals):**

1. **Claim-graph IR with mandatory provenance types.** Generation emits typed atomic claims, not prose: `QUOTE(doc, span)` · `DERIVE(premises, replayable derivation)` · `ENTAILED(α, calibration-stratum)` · `MODEL-PRIOR(p)`. Untyped assertion is unrepresentable — a type error, not a policy violation.
2. **Typed discourse edges.** Rhetorical relations (CAUSE, SEQUENCE, CONTRAST, ELABORATION) are claims too, requiring their own provenance; unlicensed causal adjacency must be explicitly cancelled in the rendered text. This closes the implicature channel that per-claim verification misses.
3. **Sound gates below the frontier.** Byte-match, derivation replay, proof kernels, executable guidelines. Deterministic, non-neural, zero-false-emission by construction (AlphaProof-style). Selector errors upstream of a sound gate cost latency and coverage, never truth.
4. **Stratified bounds above the frontier.** Mondrian (per-domain, per-claim-class) conformal calibration instead of marginal; Vovk exchangeability martingales monitoring for distribution shift, with automatic downgrade of guarantees on alarm — loud guarantee-withdrawal instead of silent guarantee-death.
5. **Judgment decomposed to shrink the residue.** A diagnosis or legal recommendation factors into findings (quotable), cited statistics (quotable), Bayesian combination (arithmetic — replayable), protocol conformance (executable guidelines), and a root prior. Only the root prior is irreducible, and it ships labeled `MODEL-PRIOR(p)` and is scored actuarially against outcomes with proper scoring rules — the same process-not-outcome epistemology malpractice law already uses.
6. **The product: the epistemic warranty.** Every output carries a machine-readable, per-claim certificate. Residual risk above the frontier is measured per stratum and is insurable. The lucrative artifact is not a non-hallucinating model — it is the certificate and the actuarial table behind it, sold as a warranty.

**Falsifiable predictions the debate committed to:**
- If claim atomization proves generation-complete (no sound discrete gate can exist), B's architecture fails (B's stake, round 1).
- If verified-only emission collapses coverage until users route around the gate, elimination fails in practice (B's stake, round 1; A's economic attack, round 2).
- If users behaviorally flatten epistemic types into plain assertion — warning fatigue — typing is cosmetic and pure bounding wins (B's stake, round 3; A endorsed, round 4).
- If per-stratum conformal calibration cannot get enough calibration data in fast-moving domains, guarantees above the frontier stay marginal and weak (open, round 3).

**Honest assessment of novelty.** Every component exists somewhere in the literature — Kalai–Vempala, conformal factuality, semantic entropy, constrained decoding, SDRT, executable clinical guidelines, selective prediction. Neither debater invented a new theorem tonight. What the debate produced that we have not seen stated elsewhere: (1) the sharp hallucination/error distinction as the *resolution* of the eliminability question, with hallucination eliminable by typing and error governed by the frontier; (2) discourse-relation typing as the answer to the implicature hole in claim-level verification; (3) the decomposition argument that most observed "judgment-domain hallucination" is actually below-frontier failure (fabricated citations, misquoted statistics, invalid arithmetic) — implying the practically achievable win is much larger than the impossibility theorems suggest; (4) the frontier itself as the correct research program and the warranty as the correct product. Whether that synthesis is worth a trillion dollars is an empirical question. It is, at minimum, a coherent and falsifiable engineering program for the problem both debaters were built from.
